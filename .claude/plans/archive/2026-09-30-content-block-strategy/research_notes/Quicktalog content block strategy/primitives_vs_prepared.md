# Primitives vs. prepared blocks in website, store and page builders: evidence for Quicktalog

_Research date: 2026-09-30. Source labels used throughout: **[Vendor]** = a company describing its own product (docs, marketing, filings, press releases); **[Independent]** = third-party reviewer, analyst, agency or researcher; **[Community]** = user forum or marketplace review posts; **[Academic]** = peer-reviewed research. Sources older than 2023 are flagged "(older: YYYY)". "(snippet)" marks a claim taken from a search-result excerpt where the full page was not retrieved: treat it as lower confidence. Everything else was read on the source page._

## 1. How do leading builders position themselves between primitives and prepared blocks, and what does each one's insert picker look like?

### Takeaway
Builders are meeting in a layered hybrid from opposite ends. Primitive-first tools (Webflow, Framer, WordPress) have added prepared sections, patterns and restricted "compose-only" roles on top of their primitives. Prepared-first tools (Shopify, Squarespace) have added freedom underneath their prepared sections (8-level nested theme blocks, a free-positioning grid), and those additions are where the loudest user pain showed up. Insert pickers now nearly all lead with categorized, previewable prepared sections or patterns, offer a blank or primitive path second, and increasingly add an AI "generate" entry.

### Inferences
- What these products share is composing a page from prepared, configurable components: Shopify presets, Squarespace section categories and auto layouts, WordPress patterns, Webflow's Marketer role (page slots plus component properties), Framer's Insert › Sections, and Durable's and Softr's blocks. Even the most primitive-heavy tools show a non-designer prepared sections first.
- Mature platforms split two tiers. A designer/developer tier builds blocks, components, presets and patterns out of primitives; a composer tier adds, orders and fills them. Quicktalog's team (and its AI agent) sits in the designer tier and the SMB owner is a composer. The founder's hypothesis amounts to "don't expose the designer tier to SMB owners", which is how Webflow, WordPress and Shopify treat their non-technical users.
- Shopify's categorized presets (one block type, several named picker entries with different defaults) and WordPress variations (Group → Row/Stack/Grid) are direct precedents for the hybrid of prepared sections with a few named variants or presets.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes how many insertions come from prepared sections versus blank or primitive paths, or any picker click-through data.
- Wix Editor's current Add panel structure (elements vs sections) and Notion's slash-menu documentation were not retrieved from primary sources in this pass.
- Hostinger's section picker details came only from review snippets.

## 2. Evidence on time-to-value and activation: templates vs blank canvas, time-to-first-publish, why vendors added prepared starts, learning-curve complaints

### Takeaway
No independent, builder-specific activation benchmark is public. The best evidence is vendor data and vendors' stated reasons, and it all points the same way. Prepared starting points (templates, sections, AI drafts) shorten time-to-publish. Primitive-first tools carry learning-curve costs that reviewers say are not worth it for time-pressed small-business owners. The most relevant numbers are Wix's 2026 figures (time-to-publish halved; restaurants from 13 to 7 days), but they are vendor-reported and don't isolate a cause.

### Cited Findings
**Benchmarks and experiments**
- [Vendor dataset, 2024] Userpilot benchmark of 547 SaaS companies: activation 34.6% for product-led and 41.6% for sales-led companies. Time-to-value is about 1 day 12 hours (product-led) and 1 day 11 hours (sales-led). Average onboarding-checklist completion is 19.2%. "80% of companies achieving activation rates above 50% use multimedia elements such as videos, GIFs, or animations." The data covers Userpilot's own customers, not website builders specifically - [Userpilot Product Metrics Benchmark Report 2024](https://userpilot.com/blog/product-metrics-benchmark-report/)
- [Vendor case study, undated, likely before 2023] Canva defines activation as "the percentage of new users who successfully create a design." Personalized onboarding sent users to poster templates matching the intent they gave at signup (a church event vs a rock concert). It raised poster activation by 10%, "tens of thousands of additional users achieving value with the product every month." It was tested on 5% of users before full rollout - [Appcues: How Canva's growth team improves activation](https://www.appcues.com/blog/canva-growth-process)
- [Vendor, 2 July 2026, "based on data from 310 million users"] "In the last year, the average time to publish a website dropped 50%, from 8 days to 4." "Restaurants are publishing websites in 7 days, down from 13 days. eCommerce businesses went from 14 days to 7. - [Vendor, SEC filings] Q1 2025: "AI continued to remove friction for more users in the website creation journey"; "Q1'25 new user cohort bookings finishing 12% higher than the bookings generated by the Q1'24 cohort in its first quarter" - [Wix Q1 2025 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1576789/000162828025026977/firstquarter2025.htm). Q2 2025: "Q2 new cohort bookings grew 14% y/y" and "improved y/y conversion across key markets, including the U.S., U.K., and Europe" - [Wix Q2 2025 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1576789/000162828025038117/secondquarter2025.htm)
- [Vendor vs Independent] Durable's own page is titled "Generate Your Website in Minutes" (snippet) - [durable.com](https://durable.com/ai-website-builder). ### Inferences
- Time-to-value in this category is mostly content work, not layout. Wix reports that 91% of users rewrite AI copy and about 70% replace images (Q5), and restaurants took 13 days (now 7) to publish on a general-purpose builder. A catalogue product whose blocks map one-to-one to content types (menu section, item, price, opening hours) takes layout decisions off that path entirely.
- The Canva result shows which templates matter. Routing by intent or business type lifted activation; a bigger generic library is not what drove it. For Quicktalog this supports vertical-specific starting catalogues (café menu, salon price list, gym timetable) and AI-built drafts from scraped or photographed menus.
- Adding freedom to a simple product produced the same pattern twice (Squarespace 2022, Shopify Horizon 2025): power users praised it, less confident users struggled, and the "simple" brand promise suffered.

### Gaps
- No independent time-to-first-publish or activation benchmark exists for website or menu builders, and no public A/B data compares prepared sections with a blank canvas in a builder.
- Several widely repeated statistics could not be traced to a primary source and were excluded: "Canva's 'Start with a template' empty state converts 75% of first sessions vs 40%", "Figma activation +34% with role-specific templates", and "template users show 3x retention". They appear only on content-marketing blogs.
- The claim that "more than 50%" or "the large majority" of new Wix users build their first site with the AI builder appears only on stats aggregators. The Q1 and Q2 2025 filings I read do not state it.
- No sourced learning-curve evidence was found for Framer or Wix Studio specifically, only general G2-style snippets.

## 3. UX research on block pickers: choice overload and Hick's law, grouping, search, recommended blocks, progressive disclosure, presets, inline vs modal editing

### Takeaway
The research does not say fewer blocks is always better. Choice overload is real but conditional: it grows with choice-set complexity, task difficulty and preference uncertainty (typical of novices), and it shrinks when options are organized, defaults exist and rare options are deferred. That argues for a small picker that is categorized, shows recommended blocks first and exposes variants as named presets. It does not argue for one universal set of primitives.

### Cited Findings
- [Academic, via secondary source] Hick's law: the time to choose among n equally likely options grows with log2(n+1) (original: Hick, 1952) - [Laws of UX: Hick's Law](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/)
- [Academic] (older: 2010) A meta-analysis of 63 conditions from 50 experiments (N = 5,036) found a "mean effect size of virtually zero but considerable variance between studies" for choice overload (snippet) - [Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd, Journal of Consumer Research 2010](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Can-There-Ever-Be-Too-Many-Options-A-Meta-Analytic-Scheibehenne-Greifeneder/07775d812c00a97aaa57b55596b39e26b8baaa0b)
- [Academic] (older: 2015) A meta-analysis of 99 observations (N = 7,202) identified four moderators - "choice set complexity, decision task difficulty, preference uncertainty, and decision goal" - that decide when assortment size causes overload. "When moderating variables are taken into account the overall effect of assortment size on choice overload is significant" (abstract snippet) - [Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman, Journal of Consumer Psychology 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057740814000916)
- [Independent] (older: 2006) NN/g on progressive disclosure: "Initially, show users only a few of the most important options" and offer specialized options on request. This "improves 3 of usability's 5 components: learnability, efficiency of use, and error rate." Pick the initial set from "frequency-of-use statistics" and "disclose everything that users frequently need up front." - [NN/g: Progressive Disclosure (Jakob Nielsen)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)
- [Independent] (older: 2015) NN/g: "An excess of choices can lead to fatigue and can make people feel dissatisfied with the experience, or even worse, abandon the process altogether." "Keeping the number of options at a reasonable level allows people to make decisions more easily and complete tasks faster." - [NN/g: Simplicity Wins over Abundance of Choice (Hoa Loranger)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/simplicity-vs-choice/). A companion NN/g video says too many offerings cause analysis paralysis or hasty choices later regretted (snippet) - [NN/g video: Choice Overload](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/choice-overload/)
- [Vendor practice] Presets expose one block type as several picker entries ("You can author multiple presets for the same theme block") - [shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/blocks/theme-blocks). Categories are used to speed finding (Shopify, May 2025) - [Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/categories-for-section-and-block-presets). WordPress offers variations (Group/Row/Stack/Grid), inserter categories, "most used" blocks, search and slash commands (snippets) - [WordPress.org: Group block](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/group-block/); [WordPress.com: blocks](https://wordpress.com/support/wordpress-editor/add-content-blocks/)
- [Vendor practice] Pickers are curated per audience. Webflow lets designers control which components Marketers see (snippet) - [Webflow Updates](https://webflow.com/updates/page-building-support-for-component-slots). Canva's +10% activation came from routing users to intent-matched templates - [Appcues](https://www.appcues.com/blog/canva-growth-process)
- [Independent, Baymard] Product-list layout should follow product type. Spec-driven products need "List View" because grid thumbnails crowd out attributes, yet 23% of benchmarked sites use Grid View for spec-driven products (snippet) - [Baymard: product listing information](https://baymard.com/blog/list-item-design-ecommerce). "78% of mobile ecommerce sites have a 'poor' to 'mediocre' performance in Product List UX" (170+ sites; published Aug 2024, updated Sep 2025) - [Baymard: current state of product lists](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-product-list-and-filtering)

### Inferences
- In Chernev's moderators, non-technical SMB owners are the high-risk group: high preference uncertainty and a harder task. Primitives raise both set complexity and task difficulty (layouts combine freely); prepared blocks lower both. That, rather than the raw number of options, is the research basis for the founder's hypothesis.
- Presets let the picker speak in outcomes ("Menu with photos", "Compact price list") while the codebase keeps one block type. This fits Baymard's finding that the right list layout depends on content type, such as photo-led café items versus price-led service lists.
- The evidence-backed mitigations are showing recommended blocks per vertical first, grouping by category, and search. Rare and advanced blocks belong behind progressive disclosure ("More" or "Advanced").

### Gaps
- No NN/g, Baymard or academic study was found that tests insert pickers in page builders specifically (picker size, grouping, search vs browse).
- No authoritative source (NN/g, Baymard or academic) was found on inline vs modal or sidebar editing in page builders. Only practitioner-blog guidance turned up (inline for small edits, dialogs for complex records), and I did not treat it as evidence.

## 4. How builders keep pages looking good when non-designers compose them, and what goes wrong with free-form composition on mobile

### Takeaway
Platforms that protect quality do it by limiting what composers can change: content-only editing, properties instead of styles, global tokens and layout variants. The biggest failure of free-form composition is mobile. Tools that allow free positioning on desktop (Squarespace Fluid Engine, Canva, Hostinger's manual mode) must either reflow automatically, which breaks designs, or keep a separate mobile layout, which doubles the work and drifts out of sync. A single-column, mobile-first flat list avoids most of this by design.

### Cited Findings
**Guardrail mechanisms**
- [Vendor/open source] (older: Oct 2022) WordPress "contentOnly" locking was added because "End users may have found they could accidentally break these layouts or use them in an unexpected way." With it, "the users can only change the nested content inside the block/pattern" and the block hierarchy is hidden - [make.wordpress.org: content locking](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2022/10/11/content-locking-features-and-updates/). A 2024 follow-up adds overrides in synced patterns: content varies per instance while structure stays shared - [developer.wordpress.org: synced pattern overrides](https://developer.wordpress.org/news/2024/06/an-introduction-to-overrides-in-synced-patterns/)
- [Vendor docs] Webflow marketers build "without breaking or deviating from your design system" by setting component properties. Without page slots they can only edit text, links and images - [Webflow Help](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961210206483-Page-building)
- [Vendor docs] Squarespace auto layouts take site-wide heading, paragraph and button styles, and switch between preset layouts without losing content - [Squarespace Help](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057763852-Auto-layouts)
- [Vendor] Shopify's Horizon ships "10 new design presets" (theme-wide style presets) - [Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/summer-25-edition-design)
- [Independent] Durable limits styling to global colours and fonts ("change global colors and fonts without touching code"), and sites are "responsive by default" - [Tooltester](https://www.tooltester.com/en/reviews/durable/). Reviewers also say Durable sites look similar to one another (snippet) - [Website Planet](https://www.websiteplanet.com/website-builders/durable/)

**Free-form composition on mobile: what goes wrong**
- [Vendor] (older: 2022) Squarespace marketed Fluid Engine's mobile grid as a feature: "design bespoke mobile landing experiences independently from a website desktop experience" - [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/squarespace-launches-fluid-engine-a-next-generation-website-design-system-301590557.html)
- [Community, 2022–2023] Users experienced it as a burden:
  - "Why do I have to make separate designs for desktop and mobile? Mobile version is terrible, never looks as intended."
  - "You need to make sure you update the mobile view by dragging the new content to it's given position after adding it in-between already existing content."
  - "It takes us back to the dark days of separate desktop and mobile sites."
  - "How about just making something responsive and letting it stack?"
  - One user told clients a course page "doesn't work in mobile view" rather than rebuild it and keep monitoring it separately.
  - [Squarespace Forum](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/224619-fluid-engine-is-trash/)
  - Related threads cover mobile edits changing the desktop view and spacing in phone landscape (snippets) - [thread](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/236796-mobile-adjustments-affecting-desktop-view/); [thread](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/223221-problems-with-phone-landscape-layout-in-fluid-engine/)
- [Community] A satisfied user's workaround: "the key is to not make an entirely fluid engine website. Just make the sections you need fluid (so maybe JUST the hero section), and the rest are regular sections." - [Squarespace Forum](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/224619-fluid-engine-is-trash/)
- [Vendor help] Canva: "On mobile view, overlapping elements will shrink, and ungrouped elements might separate." "If you want certain elements to stay together, group them. Otherwise, they might separate in mobile view." - [Canva Help: My website looks different on mobile](https://www.canva.com/help/responsive-website-issues/)
- [Vendor help] Hostinger manual mode: the mobile version shows elements "in the order they've been added to the desktop version". Later reorders on desktop "are not automatically applied to the mobile version", and mobile auto-positioning "is automatically turned off once you make any manual edits on the mobile version" (snippet) - [Hostinger support](https://support.hostinger.com/en/articles/6475658-website-builder-how-to-edit-the-mobile-version-of-a-website)
- [Independent] Wix Studio's answer is AI: design for desktop and "responsive AI" generates the tablet and mobile layouts (snippet) - [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/wix-studio-review/). Framer's Wireframer also generates breakpoints automatically (snippet) - [Framer Wireframer](https://www.framer.com/wireframer/)

### Inferences
- The mobile problems come from free 2D positioning designed on a wide canvas. They don't appear in stack-only models (Durable's reorder-only sections, Squarespace auto layouts). Quicktalog's page is already a single-column ordered list, mostly opened on phones. Adding grid, flex or column primitives would bring in exactly the failures these vendors are fighting, for little benefit on a phone-width screen.
- The strongest quality mechanisms available to Quicktalog are a stack of typed blocks, catalogue-wide theme tokens and named variants per block (for example list, grid or compact) instead of free styling. Squarespace auto layouts and Shopify presets are working precedents.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data (support-ticket share, churn) on mobile layout breakage was found for any builder. The evidence is community complaints and vendor help articles.
- Squarespace's post-2023 changes to Fluid Engine's mobile behaviour were not found in a primary source.

## 5. Does AI generation change the calculus between primitives and prepared blocks?

### Takeaway
AI removes much of the blank-canvas cost for both models, so the difference shifts to what happens after generation: reliable editing, validation and consistency. The AI editing vendors ship widely either edits the settings of existing prepared sections and blocks (Shopify Sidekick) or generates native components on a stable platform (Wix Harmony). Free code generation (Shopify AI blocks, Framer Workshop) comes with explicit quality and support disclaimers. For a product whose agent must reliably edit structured content, a closed schema of prepared blocks is the more AI-friendly foundation.

### Cited Findings
- [Vendor help] Shopify AI blocks. Generation creates a new Liquid file containing "HTML, CSS, JSON, and JavaScript". "The liquid file generated by Shopify Magic is self-contained." Merchants can refine a block with "follow-up instructions" and reuse it from the picker. Shopify warns: "AI-generated code may not always be perfect. It can sometimes produce errors, unexpected results, or code that doesn't fully meet accessibility or performance best practices." "Assistance with customizing or troubleshooting the code for blocks generated by Shopify Magic isn't supported by Shopify Support and third-party theme developers." It works on Theme Store themes (not vintage themes) and not on the Starter plan - [Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/theme-editor/shopify-magic/generate-blocks)
- [Vendor, 10 Dec 2025] Sidekick in the theme editor: "Customize any store element through natural language commands like 'make this button rounded.'" Merchants can "click on anything in the theme editor and ask Sidekick to make the edit for you"; it covers global styles and block settings - [Shopify Winter '26 Edition](https://www.shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-merchant)
- [Independent, updated 14 Sep 2026] Sidekick works at the settings level. It can "change existing theme settings (colors, fonts, spacing, layout settings) and move, remove, or hide sections that are already there." It "doesn't write Liquid or CSS", "doesn't create new section types" and "can't build layouts your theme doesn't support." - [Fudge.ai Sidekick guide](https://www.fudge.ai/guides/shopify-sidekick-use-cases/)
- [Vendor, Jan 2026] Wix Harmony's Aria "understands natural language and the full context of a site", handling tasks "from simple tasks like updating color palettes to complex ones like redesigning an entire page." It produces "native components with built-in logic and code, fully editable alongside manual design tools." Wix dismisses vibe-coding tools as producing "quick demos or lightweight MVPs" - [Wix press release](https://www.wix.com/press-room/home/post/wix-launches-wix-harmony-the-ai-website-builder-that-merges-human-and-artificial-intelligence-rein)
- [Vendor, July 2026] After AI generation, people still edit the content: "Ninety-one percent of users rewrite their own copy, and nearly 70% swap out images for their own photos." "Most users tap an AI agent for help at some point in the process." - [Wix report](https://www.wix.com/blog/how-is-ai-changing-website-creation)
- [Vendor, May 2025] Framer's Wireframer generates page structures from prompts, which users then adjust. Workshop generates custom code components - [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250521574932/en/Framer-Launches-AI-Features-to-Supercharge-Web-Design-Democratizing-How-Stunning-Websites-are-Built)
- [Independent] Durable generates a site from a few inputs, but editing is limited to pre-made sections; you "can remove fields from a form, but you cannot add new ones" - [Tooltester](https://www.tooltester.com/en/reviews/durable/)
- [Vendor/technical, 6 Aug 2024] Schema-constrained generation: "On our evals of complex JSON schema following, our new model gpt-4o-2024-08-06 with Structured Outputs scores a perfect 100%. In comparison, gpt-4-0613 scores less than 40%." - [OpenAI: Introducing Structured Outputs](https://openai.com/index/introducing-structured-outputs-in-the-api/)

### Inferences
- AI weakens the blank-canvas argument against primitives, since Framer and Wix can now draft layouts. It strengthens the case for prepared blocks wherever reliability matters. A closed set of block types with typed fields can be enforced at generation time (structured outputs), validated, diffed and undone. Free layout trees or generated code cannot be validated the same way, and Shopify doesn't even support its own AI-generated code.
- Shopify's split maps well onto Quicktalog: AI creates new block types as isolated, self-contained files, while everyday AI edits change the settings of existing blocks. Quicktalog's agent can emit and edit typed block JSON (block type, variant enum, content fields) against the catalogue schema, and the Quicktalog team can use AI internally to add new prepared block types quickly.
- Because 91% of Wix users rewrite the AI's copy, the editor used after generation still determines time-to-value. Prepared blocks with simple content forms, rather than layout controls, keep that editing loop short.

### Gaps
- No builder publishes reliability metrics for its AI editing (edit success rate, undo rate, error rate).
- Third-party claims that Shopify's generator is English-only, cannot iterate on a block after the editor reloads, or slows pages down (snippets from eesel.ai- and Craftshift-type blogs) could not be verified. Shopify's help page mentions refinement through follow-up instructions, which partly contradicts the "no iteration" claim.

## 6. Where prepared-block products hit a ceiling, and how they offer an escape hatch without it becoming the main path

### Takeaway
Prepared-block products hit ceilings in three places: layouts look alike ("sites look similar"), blocks can't be configured far enough (you can't add a form field), and per-page limits. Successful platforms keep escape hatches deliberately off the main path. The options are a clearly labelled code or embed section, AI-generated custom blocks stored as isolated files, or a designer tier that creates new prepared components. Custom code is often explicitly unsupported.

### Cited Findings
- [Independent] Durable's ceilings: no free positioning ("the editor uses pre‑made section blocks"), a maximum of 20 sections per page, and form fields can be removed but not added - [Tooltester](https://www.tooltester.com/en/reviews/durable/)
- [Vendor help] Flexible platforms have page limits too. Squarespace recommends at most 20 sections per page for load time - [Squarespace Help](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360027987711-Page-sections). Canva publishes at most 10 sections per page on Free and 20 on paid plans - [Canva Help](https://www.canva.com/help/responsive-website-issues/)
- [Independent] Shopify's Custom Liquid section is "the escape hatch for theme code", used for tracking pixels, third-party widgets and small CTAs, and added via Add section › Custom Liquid (snippets) - [Kaspian Fuad](https://kaspianfuad.com/blog/shopify-custom-liquid-section-script-tags/); [EComposer](https://ecomposer.io/blogs/news/shopify-custom-liquid-section)
- [Vendor help] Shopify treats AI-generated blocks as self-contained files whose code is supported by neither Shopify Support nor theme developers - [Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/theme-editor/shopify-magic/generate-blocks)
- [Vendor docs] In Webflow, new layouts require the designer tier. Marketers can only add existing components, and without page slots they can only edit content - [Webflow Help](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961210206483-Page-building)
- [Vendor] (older: 2022) Squarespace justified Fluid Engine as reaching designs "without having to resort to custom code", which implies the earlier section model's ceiling was pushing users into code - [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/squarespace-launches-fluid-engine-a-next-generation-website-design-system-301590557.html)
- [Community] Softr users ask how to restyle prepared blocks with CSS (thread titles; snippet) - [Softr Community: Customizing blocks?](https://community.softr.io/t/customizing-blocks/1410); [Softr Community: table block CSS](https://community.softr.io/t/how-to-modify-dynamic-table-block-look-and-feel-css/3097)
- [Community] A hybrid workaround: use free-form sections only where needed (the hero) and prepared sections elsewhere - [Squarespace Forum](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/224619-fluid-engine-is-trash/)

### Inferences
- Squarespace's history shows the risk of reacting to a ceiling. When prepared sections felt limiting, the company replaced them with a free grid for everyone, with no way back, and that hurt its core non-designer users. Shopify and Webflow follow a safer pattern: the prepared path stays the default, and rare needs go to (a) a narrowly scoped embed or code block, or (b) new prepared block types built centrally by the team, with AI help. Custom code is marked unsupported.
- Quicktalog's most likely ceiling requests are embeds (maps, booking or reservations, delivery links, social feeds) and a few bespoke layouts. An allow-listed Embed block plus a fast pipeline for new block types and variants probably covers them without a primitive system.

### Gaps
- No data on what share of Shopify stores use Custom Liquid, or how often Squarespace users use code blocks.
- Plan gating for Squarespace's Code Block and Carrd's Embed element was not verified from primary sources.

## 7. Test of the founder's hypothesis: which model minimises friction and time-to-value for Quicktalog, and under what conditions does each model win?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports the hypothesis for Quicktalog's users and page shape, with one refinement. Build prepared blocks with a few named variants or presets, composed internally from nested sub-blocks so the team and the AI agent can create and evolve them. Expose them to SMB owners as flat, content-only blocks styled by theme tokens. The evidence against the hypothesis is real, but it applies mainly to design-led users, desktop marketing pages and brand differentiation. Those conditions are mostly absent in catalogues opened on a phone from a QR code.

### Cited Findings
**For prepared blocks (supports the hypothesis)**
- Even professional primitive tools add prepared layers for speed and for non-designers: Webflow's layouts (2020) and Marketer page building, Framer's Sections (2022) and WordPress's Zoom Out patterns (2024) - [Webflow Updates](https://webflow.com/updates/build-faster-with-prebuilt-layouts); [Webflow Help](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961210206483-Page-building); [Framer Updates](https://www.framer.com/updates/sections); [make.wordpress.org](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2024/10/21/developer-notes-for-zoom-out-in-wordpress-6-7/)
- Adding freedom to a simple product caused visible pain. Squarespace's Fluid Engine drew a backlash over separate mobile designs, shifting blocks and doubled editing time - [Squarespace Forum](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/224619-fluid-engine-is-trash/). Shopify's Horizon has a 35% positive rating and hides dependent settings behind toggles - [Theme Store](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/horizon/styles/horizon); [Craftshift](https://craftshift.com/shopify-horizon-theme-hidden-settings-tips-2026/)
- Reviewers judge primitive-first tools' learning curves "not worth climbing" for most small-business owners - [Toolfoundry](https://www.toolfoundry.co.uk/reviews/webflow-review-2026); [The Designer Review](https://thedesignerreview.com/webflow-for-beginners/)
- Canva's and Hostinger's own help pages document mobile breakage from free-form layouts - [Canva Help](https://www.canva.com/help/responsive-website-issues/); [Hostinger support](https://support.hostinger.com/en/articles/6475658-website-builder-how-to-edit-the-mobile-version-of-a-website)
- Closed, structured schemas make AI output reliable (100% vs under 40% schema adherence) - [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-structured-outputs-in-the-api/)
- The choice-overload moderators (preference uncertainty, task difficulty) are strongest for novices - [Chernev et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057740814000916)

**Against, or limits of, the hypothesis**
- Users ask for "more expression, more control"; this is Shopify's stated reason for Horizon - [Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/summer-25-edition-design)
- Prepared-only builders get criticized as rigid, as producing sites that look similar, and for limits like not being able to add a form field - [Tooltester](https://www.tooltester.com/en/reviews/durable/); [Website Planet](https://www.websiteplanet.com/website-builders/durable/)
- Some users love free-form grids and get better-looking results with them - [Squarespace Forum](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/224619-fluid-engine-is-trash/)
- Across studies, choice overload averages close to zero effect, so a larger picker is not harmful in itself if it is well organized - [Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Can-There-Ever-Be-Too-Many-Options-A-Meta-Analytic-Scheibehenne-Greifeneder/07775d812c00a97aaa57b55596b39e26b8baaa0b)
- AI (Framer Wireframer, Wix Harmony, Wix Studio's responsive AI) is cutting the setup and breakpoint costs of primitives - [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250521574932/en/Framer-Launches-AI-Features-to-Supercharge-Web-Design-Democratizing-How-Stunning-Websites-are-Built); [Wix press release](https://www.wix.com/press-room/home/post/wix-launches-wix-harmony-the-ai-website-builder-that-merges-human-and-artificial-intelligence-rein); [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/wix-studio-review/)
- Carrd suggests constrained primitives (one page, containers of up to five columns) can stay simple - [Carrd docs](https://carrd.com/docs/building/organizing-elements-into-columns)

### Inferences
- **Primitives win when:**
  - users are designers or agencies, or have one (the audiences of Webflow, Framer and Wix Studio);
  - brand differentiation is the product;
  - pages are desktop-first with 2D compositions;
  - content is updated rarely;
  - a designer tier can set guardrails for everyone else.
- **Prepared blocks win when:**
  - owners are non-technical and short on time;
  - pages are content-driven and edited often (prices, availability, hours);
  - the page is mobile-first and single-column;
  - the vertical has predictable content types (menu sections, services, price lists, schedules);
  - an AI agent must generate and edit reliably;
  - brand is expressed through theme tokens rather than layout.

  Quicktalog matches nearly all of these.
- **The hybrid that best fits the evidence for Quicktalog:**
  1. Prepared blocks are the only thing users insert. The picker is grouped by category, shows recommended blocks per business type first, and is searchable, with rare or advanced blocks behind "More" (NN/g progressive disclosure; Shopify categories; Canva's intent routing).
  2. Variants and presets appear as separate named picker entries backed by one block type, with switching that keeps content (Shopify presets, WordPress variations, Squarespace auto layouts).
  3. Internally, blocks may be built from nested sub-blocks, Horizon-style. Owners only edit content and never see the tree (WordPress contentOnly; Webflow's Marketer role). Horizon's merchant confusion shows the cost of exposing nesting.
  4. Theme tokens own the styling. Per block, owners get a variant switch and no free styling.
  5. The AI agent writes typed block JSON against a closed schema (variant enums, content fields). New block types are authored centrally, with AI help, and shipped as presets. This is Shopify's "generate a block type once, reuse it from the picker" pattern without exposing code to owners.
  6. The escape hatch is an allow-listed Embed block in an Advanced category, with no raw layout primitives.
- **What went right and wrong for the builders studied:**
  - Went right: Squarespace auto layouts (content entered once, switchable layouts); Shopify presets and categories; WordPress patterns, Zoom Out and contentOnly locking; Webflow's page building for Marketers; Shopify Sidekick's settings-level AI edits; Durable's speed to a first draft.
  - Went wrong: Squarespace forcing Fluid Engine on users with no way back and with separate mobile layouts; mobile reflow problems in Canva and Hostinger's free-form editors; Horizon's deep nesting, conditional settings and unstable releases (35% positive); Shopify's unsupported AI-generated code; Webflow's learning curve for SMBs.

### Gaps
- No controlled study compares primitives and prepared blocks for SMB owners on time-to-publish or retention. The recommendation rests on converging vendor behaviour, community evidence and general HCI findings.
- Menu- and catalogue-specific builders (e.g., restaurant menu tools) and their picker designs were outside this pass and may be covered by a parallel researcher.

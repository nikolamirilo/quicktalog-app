# Trade escape hatches for prepared business blocks

Quicktalog's builder has one genuinely structured block, `items`, and four stopgaps around it: a text editor that cannot make a link or a heading, a horizontal rule that is the Basic plan's only block feature, an iframe box that expects pasted embed code, and a sandboxed code box. Everything a visitor looks for beyond the price list (opening hours, an address, a button to call, message or book, a promotion, allergen information) has to be typed as prose or pasted as code, and the code paths fail silently for most of the widgets small businesses actually use. The founder's lean toward prepared solutions survives the evidence: builders at both ends of the market are converging on prepared blocks with named variants, the two vendors that pushed layout freedom into simple products drew backlash from their less confident users, free-form layouts break on phones, closed schemas are what make an AI agent reliable, and small-business owners spend under five hours a week on all of their marketing. The proposed three-way split is half right. A URL-first embed with purpose-named entries (video, map, social post, booking) is the industry pattern; tracking should not be a component at all but a catalogue setting behind a consent banner, because neither rendering path can run a tag and no surveyed platform puts one in a block; and banners need a native no-code block, because a button inside the custom-code sandbox cannot navigate anywhere. The best lineup, in order: make `items` truthful for services and food (no more "Free" for unpriced items, service units, allergen badges) and give text links and headings; add Hours & Location, Buttons and Banner blocks on every plan; rebuild the embed as URL-first; then add a gallery, an FAQ, highlight badges and per-item actions; ship GA4 and Meta Pixel as a setting only once consent exists; and keep custom code as an honestly labelled widget sandbox. Quicktalog's own usage is unmeasured (the TEST database is empty and production was not queried), so a read-only production classification should confirm this order before the build starts.

## One structured block and four stopgaps carry every catalogue today

Code references in this report point to the `quicktalog-app` repository at commit `d785bb8` and to `quicktalog-packages` 1.66.0; market claims link to their public sources. Quicktalog has **five writable block types**: `items`, `text`, `divider`, `embedding` (labelled "External content" in the picker) and `custom_code`, with the legacy `category` and `container` keys converted to `items` on read ([content-blocks.ts:12-64](../../../../../src/lib/catalogue/content-blocks.ts#L12-L64)). Blocks share only an `id` and an `order` ([catalogue.ts:46-49](../../../../../../quicktalog-packages/src/types/catalogue.ts#L46-L49)), and the page is one flat, ordered list rendered by a hand-written if/else chain, with no nesting, no columns, no stable anchors and no per-block presentation settings ([CatalogueContent.tsx:237-373](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/view/CatalogueContent.tsx#L237-L373)). Only `items` carries structured data. Anything that is not a priced list (an intro, hours, contact details, an FAQ, a promotion, a team, a gallery) has to become prose in a text block, or third-party or custom code on the Pro and Growth plans.

Real-world usage is thin evidence. The TEST database holds one catalogue whose content is empty, and production was deliberately not queried. The seven showcase catalogues on Quicktalog's marketing site contain **36 blocks: 31 item sections and 5 text blocks, and not one divider, embed or custom-code block**. All five text blocks are an unnamed welcome paragraph placed first, all 200 items carry a photo, and all seven pages run the default Quicktalog-branded header and footer ([showcase parse, 1 Oct 2026](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_blocks_inventory.md); [marketing.ts:7-16](../../../../../src/constants/marketing.ts#L7-L16)). The sample is curated and retail-heavy, with one restaurant and no salons or gyms, so it shows what the product makes easy more than what customers need. It is nonetheless consistent with the scorecard below.

| Block (plan) | Serves well | Fails customers | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Items (all plans) | Priced products with photos, in four layouts; used by 7 of 7 showcases | A blank price saves as 0 and shows "Free"; no service units, variants, allergen or highlight badges; the detail modal shows the pre-discount price | Strong for products, misleading for services |
| Text (all plans) | The intro or "about" paragraph, its only observed use | No links, headings or images; paste strips formatting; three of four text sizes never publish; the only home for a Starter owner's hours and phone number | Adequate for intros, poor at everything else it is forced to do |
| Divider (Basic+) | Whitespace or a rule | Black by default on dark themes; the AI cannot restyle it; unused in the showcases | Low value, and the wrong thing to charge for |
| External content (Pro+) | Google Maps, YouTube, Vimeo, Spotify, Google Forms, Calendly's iframe | Expects pasted code; URLs render as text; script embeds and unlisted hosts vanish silently; YouTube renders 400 px tall on a phone | Right engine, wrong input |
| Custom code (Growth+) | Sandboxed, AI-built games, calculators and animations | Links, forms, pop-ups and tracking are blocked; content is silently clipped at 5,000 px; no preview in the dialog | Right only for interactive widgets |

**Items sells products well and mislabels services.** Four card layouts, collapsible headings and paging make it a capable menu engine, and it is the one block every showcase uses. But no authoring path can create an item without a price. The editor stores a blank price as 0 ([ItemPricing.tsx:57-72](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/inputs/item/ItemPricing.tsx#L57-L72)), the AI defaults a missing price to 0 and is told to "Use 0 when there is no price" ([operations.ts:119](../../../../../src/lib/catalogue/operations.ts#L119); [instructions.ts:95](../../../../../src/agent/instructions.ts#L95)), and the card renders 0 as **"Free"** without ever reading the `isFree` flag ([cards/index.tsx:65-86](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/cards/index.tsx#L65-L86)). The unit list stops at kg, g, l, ml, pcs and portion, with no hour, session, person, night or minute ([ItemPricing.tsx:20-28](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/inputs/item/ItemPricing.tsx#L20-L28)). A salon's "price on request" therefore publishes as "Free", "from €30" and "45 min" survive only as description text, and three hair lengths become three items. The detail modal adds a correctness bug: for a discounted item it shows the original price and drops the unit, contradicting the card on published pages ([ItemDetailModal.tsx:78-90](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/modals/ItemDetailModal.tsx#L78-L90)). Marketing promises what the model cannot hold. The restaurant guide answers "Can I handle allergens and dietary tags? Yes" ([digital-menu-for-restaurants.tsx:283-287](../../../../../src/content/articles/digital-menu-for-restaurants.tsx#L283-L287)), and the salon guide promises durations, packages and tiered pricing ([digital-service-menu-salons-spas.tsx:86-100](../../../../../src/content/articles/digital-service-menu-salons-spas.tsx#L86-L100)), yet the `Item` type has a field for none of them ([catalogue.ts:126-142](../../../../../../quicktalog-packages/src/types/catalogue.ts#L126-L142)).

**Text carries the intro and little else.** The toolbar offers bold, italic, underline, lists, alignment and a size picker, but no link, heading, image or colour, and paste flattens everything to plain text ([RichTextEditor.tsx:85-98](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/common/RichTextEditor.tsx#L85-L98)). The size picker is silently broken on published pages: it writes `<font size>` tags that the sanitizer strips, so Small, Large and Extra Large never publish. All five showcase text blocks store `<font size="5">`, and their rendered pages contain no `<font>` tag at all ([RichTextEditor.tsx:238-253](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/common/RichTextEditor.tsx#L238-L253); [sanitize.ts:59-104](../../../../../src/lib/html/sanitize.ts#L59-L104)). Meanwhile the dialog advertises "headings, lists, links, and formatting" ([BlockConfigHeader.tsx:32](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/modals/content/BlockConfigHeader.tsx#L32)). The sanitizer's text profile already allows headings, links, images and tables, so the gap is purely in the toolbar. It matters more than it looks, because on the Starter plan a text block is the **only place an owner can publish their own phone number, address or hours**. Contact fields, the logo and the custom header and footer all sit behind the Basic plan's branding flag, and the default header shows Quicktalog's own email address and a "Create Your Catalog" sign-up button instead ([GeneralTab.tsx:45-163](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/inputs/sidebar/GeneralTab.tsx#L45-L163); [CatalogueHeader.tsx:74-124](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/view/CatalogueHeader.tsx#L74-L124)).

**The divider is whitespace sold as an upgrade.** It renders a bare `<div>` whose default border is black regardless of theme, although three of the seven standard themes are dark, and the AI can rename a divider but not restyle it ([DividerBlock.tsx:35-52](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/DividerBlock.tsx#L35-L52); [operations.ts:265-305](../../../../../src/lib/catalogue/operations.ts#L265-L305)). It is the only block feature of the cheapest paid plan ([pricing.ts](../../../../../../quicktalog-packages/src/constants/pricing.ts)), and none of the showcases uses it.

**The embed block has the right engine and the wrong input.** Owners paste raw embed code into a textarea, and the five "presets" (Maps, Booking, Media, Commerce, Social Media) only change the placeholder text and are never stored ([EmbeddingInput.tsx:20-59](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/inputs/EmbeddingInput.tsx#L20-L59)). At render, a sanitizer keeps iframes from 37 allowlisted host suffixes and drops every `<script>` ([sanitize.ts:14-57](../../../../../src/lib/html/sanitize.ts#L14-L57)). When 30 real vendor snippets were run through that sanitizer, Google Maps, YouTube, Vimeo, Spotify, the Instagram and Facebook iframe variants, Calendly's iframe and Google Forms survived. Instagram, TikTok and X share codes degraded to a static quote, Calendly's default code left an empty 700-pixel box, and OpenTable, Typeform, Tally, the Stripe Buy Button and Elfsight vanished; a pasted URL rendered as literal text ([sanitizer probe](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_embed_custom_code.md)). Nothing tells the owner, because failed markup simply renders an empty container ([EmbeddingBlock.tsx:199-213](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/EmbeddingBlock.tsx#L199-L213)), and the placeholder copy even names OpenTable, Stripe and Shopify. Sizing is guessed by a regex over the iframe URL that misses YouTube's standard `/embed/` address, so a YouTube video renders **400 pixels tall on a 360-pixel phone, roughly 200 pixels of black bars**, while Calendly and Google Forms are squeezed to 400 pixels ([EmbeddingBlock.tsx:18-132](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/EmbeddingBlock.tsx#L18-L132)). A latent outage sits underneath. The Content-Security-Policy header is Report-Only, and its `frame-src` covers only Paddle subdomains, so **enforcing it as written would break 36 of the 37 embed hosts** ([next.config.ts:48-67](../../../../../next.config.ts#L48-L67)).

**Custom code is a good widget sandbox and nothing else.** Owners on Growth and above, or the AI where the plan allows, write HTML, CSS and JavaScript that runs inside `<iframe sandbox="allow-scripts" srcdoc>`, an opaque origin with no access to cookies, storage, the parent page or the real URL ([CustomCode.tsx:54-92](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/CustomCode.tsx#L54-L92); [CustomCode.tsx:199](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/CustomCode.tsx#L199)). That suits the self-contained games and calculators the agent is told to write ([instructions.ts:57-61](../../../../../src/agent/instructions.ts#L57-L61)). It defeats the step most business widgets end with. In a Chromium probe, new-tab and top-level links, form submissions and pop-ups were all blocked, a plain link loaded its destination inside the widget's own box, and `tel:` and `mailto:` links did nothing; real phones were not tested ([sandbox probe](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_embed_custom_code.md)). The frame starts at 200 pixels and is silently clipped at 5,000 ([CustomCode.tsx:14-16](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/sections/CustomCode.tsx#L14-L16)), the code dialog has no live preview, and the Monaco editor loads from a CDN that the CSP's `script-src` does not list. Until 15 September 2026 this block ran code first-party in the page, so any tracking or widget script an owner pasted before then has since stopped working or now runs blind ([git history](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_embed_custom_code.md)).

**The builder makes page structure expensive.** Apart from the AI chat, the only entry point is an "Add Section" tile at the very bottom of the page. Every new block is appended at the end and moves up one position per click, because the `addBlock(block, index)` helper that could insert in place has no callers ([AddContentModal.tsx:198-205](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/modals/AddContentModal.tsx#L198-L205); [CatalogueContext.tsx:94-105](../../../../../src/context/CatalogueContext.tsx#L94-L105)). There is no drag-and-drop, duplicate or undo. The AI agent can insert at any position, so the chat is strictly more capable than the manual editor at structuring a page ([sections.ts:77-79](../../../../../src/agent/tools/sections.ts#L77-L79)). The three templates are generic and items-only, and the "Standard Catalog" template uses exactly Starter's limits of five sections and 15 items, so a new Starter user who picks it hits both caps at once ([catalogueTemplates.ts](../../../../../src/constants/catalogueTemplates.ts)). Underneath, block-type gating lives only in the picker and the AI path. The server actions that save and publish accept any block type, so nothing on the server stops a Starter account from publishing `custom_code`, and sections are counted by two incompatible rules in four places ([actions/catalogue.ts:265-275](../../../../../src/actions/catalogue.ts#L265-L275); [plan.ts:86-135](../../../../../src/lib/entitlements/plan.ts#L86-L135)).

## The proposed split holds for embeds but fails for tracking and banners

The two code blocks already embody two different trust models. `embedding` renders sanitized markup inside the page itself, where links work and scripts never run; `custom_code` runs anything inside an isolated frame, where scripts run and nothing can leave. Neither can run a tracking tag with page context, and neither can host a banner whose button both works and needs no code. The founder's three components therefore map onto three different shapes, and only one of them is a block as proposed. Splitting `custom_code` into several purpose-named code blocks would change nothing, because every one would share the same sandbox and the same limits. The split is worth doing precisely because the three jobs need three different trust models.

| Proposed component | Verdict | Recommended shape | Deciding evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Embed for maps and social posts | Yes, re-scoped | One URL-first embed engine exposed as named entries (Video, Map, Social post, Booking, Form), click-to-load by default | Industry standard; removes paste-code failures; social share scripts can never run under the no-script rule |
| Tracking component (GTM, GA) | Not a block; build a setting | Validated GA4 and Meta Pixel IDs at catalogue level, rendered first-party after consent; GTM only after catalogues move to a separate domain | Neither block can run a tag; every surveyed platform uses settings; catalogue pages have no consent handling; a GTM container is arbitrary first-party JavaScript |
| Custom code for banners and custom HTML | Split differently | A native Banner block for banners; sanitized static HTML as the embed's advanced entry; the sandbox renamed "Interactive widget" | Sandboxed buttons cannot navigate; builders ship banners as no-code components |

### Embeds: one engine with purpose-named entries

The market has settled on three layers: paste a share link and let the platform detect the provider, use purpose-built blocks for the highest-volume content, and keep raw embed code as a separate, gated escape hatch. Notion recognises embeddable URLs from over 1,900 domains through Iframely and adds preset blocks such as `/maps` and `/tweet` ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/embed-and-connect-other-apps)). Squarespace's embed block takes an oEmbed URL first and a code snippet second, and tells users "For most YouTube and Vimeo videos, use the video block instead" ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206543617-Embed-blocks)). Webflow's Video element takes a URL from a fixed provider list ([Webflow](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961304305427-Video)), and Linktree offers curated link apps with no raw code at all ([Linktree](https://linktr.ee/help/en/articles/5434140-an-overview-of-paid-features-available-on-linktree)). No surveyed product makes "paste iframe code" its main embed input for mainstream providers.

Quicktalog should build one embed engine that accepts a share link, resolves provider, embed URL, aspect ratio and minimum height on the server, and builds the iframe itself. The picker then exposes that engine as several named entries (Video, Map, Social post, Booking, Form, Music), the pattern Shopify uses when one block type appears as several picker entries with different defaults ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/blocks/theme-blocks)). This gives owners the purpose-specific components the founder wants without multiplying renderers. Social posts need URL-to-iframe conversion because their script share codes can never run; Instagram's and Facebook's iframe endpoints already pass the allowlist, while TikTok's and X's still need verifying. One host list should generate both the sanitizer allowlist and CSP `frame-src`, and an unsupported link should produce a message naming the host instead of a blank box. The default render should be a click-to-load facade. Lazy-loading saves about 500 KB for a YouTube embed and 514 KB for a Spotify embed ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/iframe-lazy-loading)), which matters on a phone opened from a table QR code. It also keeps third-party requests off page load, which is where the CJEU's Fashion ID ruling makes a site operator jointly responsible for the data an embedded plugin collects and transmits ([CJEU C-40/17](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2019-07/cp190099en.pdf)). No surveyed builder documents consent-gated embeds, so this would put Quicktalog ahead of the market rather than level with it.

Booking is the weakest fit for an embed. Most small-business booking tools are off the allowlist or script-only (Fresha, OpenTable, Cal.com and Typeform all failed), so the universal answer is a "Book" button, and Fresha itself hands owners a booking link for exactly this purpose ([Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/online-profile/152-add-a-book-button-to-your-website)). Where a provider offers an iframe, as Calendly does, embed it: Linktree claims creators using its Calendly app "are generating 7x more bookings than those with a regular link out" ([Linktree](https://linktr.ee/marketplace/apps/calendly)), a vendor figure, but a reason to embed where the provider allows it. Video has only stated-preference support (82% of US adults in a Popmenu survey said they are more likely to order a dish shown with video) ([Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/05/popmenu-unveils-video-first-menu-experience-designed-to-drive-more-restaurant-traffic-and-orders/)), which places it behind the essentials in the lineup below. Because the platform builds the iframe from a parsed URL, the curated entries carry no author markup and no longer need to sit at Pro. Squarespace offers embed blocks on all plans ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205815928-Add-custom-code-to-your-site)) and Taplink unlocks video on its $4 tier ([Taplink](https://taplink.at/en/blog/what-is-taplink.html)); moving curated embeds to Basic would give that plan a real block feature in place of the divider.

### Tracking: typed IDs behind a consent gate, never a block

Neither block can carry a tracking tag, and this was verified in Chromium rather than inferred. The embed sanitizer deletes GTM and GA4 snippets outright. Inside the custom-code sandbox, the real GA4 `gtag.js` loaded but sent **no hits at all** over two page loads, while the same tag running first-party on the same page reported the real URL and set its cookies; the Meta Pixel sent PageViews with the location `about:srcdoc`, an empty referrer and no browser id ([GA4 and Pixel probes](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_embed_custom_code.md)). A tag in the sandbox sees no page URL, no UTM parameters, no persistent visitor id and no clicks outside its own box. A tracking block would add three problems of its own: it would be orderable, it would count toward the section limit, and the AI agent could write it.

The market agrees on the shape. None of the 13 website, store and link-in-bio builders surveyed puts tracking in a positional content block; Carrd's "Hidden" embed is technically an element, but it publishes outside the page flow ([Carrd](https://carrd.co/docs/building/embedding-custom-code)). The convention is a settings page with one validated field per vendor, usually on a mid paid tier. Linktree takes a GA4 Measurement ID and a Meta Pixel ID on Pro and Premium ([Linktree GA](https://help.linktr.ee/en/articles/5434201-google-analytics-integration); [Linktree Pixel](https://help.linktr.ee/en/articles/5434194-facebook-meta-pixel-integration)). Carrd offers analytics plus Meta, TikTok and LinkedIn trackers from Pro Standard ([Carrd](https://carrd.com/docs/sites/setting-up-marketing-trackers)). Squarespace connects GA4 by its "G-" ID and ties it to its cookie banner's consent mode ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205815608-Connect-Google-Analytics-to-your-site)), and Wix groups Google Tag, GTM, Meta, TikTok and Google Ads under Marketing Integrations on paid plans with a connected domain ([Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/about-marketing-integrations)). Quicktalog should store validated GA4 and Meta Pixel IDs in a catalogue-level setting, emit the tags first-party from the published catalogue page (not from a route layout, which would also wrap the owner's signed-in preview), and keep the setting out of every AI tool schema.

Consent has to come first, and it is missing even for Quicktalog's own tags. The cookie banner returns early on any path containing `/catalogues/` ([CookieBanner.tsx:28-39](../../../../../src/components/general/CookieBanner.tsx#L28-L39)), so Quicktalog's GTM container, Microsoft Clarity, PostHog page-view capture and Sentry's error replay load for every catalogue visitor with no consent step ([layout.tsx:46-49](../../../../../src/app/layout.tsx#L46-L49); [instrumentation-client.ts:5-42](../../../../../src/instrumentation-client.ts#L5-L42)). Elsewhere in the app, the "consent" signal is a custom dataLayer event rather than Google Consent Mode. EU law is clear about what a customer's pixel would need. The EDPB's Guidelines 2/2023 bring tracking pixels, and the JavaScript that builds them, under the ePrivacy consent rule ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)); the CJEU's Planet49 judgment requires active opt-in ([CJEU C-673/17](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2019-10/cp190125en.pdf)); and Google requires Consent Mode v2 signals for EEA measurement, ad personalisation and remarketing ([Google](https://support.google.com/tagmanager/answer/13695607?hl=en)). The sequence is therefore an identity-free consent banner on catalogue pages that sets consent defaults before any tag loads, and only then the tracking setting. Shipping the setting without the banner would make Quicktalog a party to unlawful processing for its EU customers. The CSP also needs `connect.facebook.net`, and the one tracking path that works today, a Meta `noscript` image beacon that survives the embed sanitizer and fires an unconsented PageView, should be closed or knowingly accepted at the same time ([sanitizer probe](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_embed_custom_code.md)). Owners already see daily views and unique visitors from the built-in PostHog analytics ([analytics.md](../../../../../docs/architecture/analytics.md)), so demand for GA4 and the Pixel is really about ad attribution and remarketing, where consent matters most.

Google Tag Manager is the hard case. A container ID points at remotely editable JavaScript that would run first-party on the same origin as the signed-in builder, and the CSP already allows `'unsafe-inline'` and `*.googletagmanager.com`, so it could not contain what the container loads ([next.config.ts:48-62](../../../../../next.config.ts#L48-L62)). GTM also has an abuse record: Recorded Future linked skimmer infections on 569 e-commerce domains to GTM abuse, 314 of them confirmed ([Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/research/threat-actors-continue-to-abuse-google-tag-manager-for-payment-card-e-skimming)), and Sucuri found known-malicious containers on 327 sites in the first eleven months of 2023 ([The Record](https://therecord.media/web-skimmers-hit-300-sites-hidden-inside-google-tag-manager-containers)). The platforms that support GTM mostly serve customer pages from separate registrable domains on the Public Suffix List, such as myshopify.com, webflow.io, framer.app, wixsite.com and carrd.co; Webflow's and Framer's entries were filed by their security teams ([Public Suffix List](https://publicsuffix.org/list/public_suffix_list.dat)). That is the isolation GitHub adopted in 2013 when it moved Pages off github.com over related-domain cookie attacks ([GitHub](https://github.blog/2013-04-05-new-github-pages-domain-github-io/)). Linktree, whose public pages share linktr.ee with its admin, documents only GA4 and Pixel IDs, and Shopify runs GTM only inside a sandbox with a purpose-built bridge to the page ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/pixels/custom-pixels/gtm-tutorial)). GTM, and any free-form head-code field, should wait for a deliberate decision to serve public catalogues from a separate domain.

### Banners get a native block; custom code becomes the widget sandbox

Banners, announcement bars and promotions are no-code, theme-bound components throughout the market. Shopify's announcement bar holds up to 12 rotating announcements ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/common-customizations/add-announcement-banner)). Squarespace's promotional pop-up offers a headline, body, image, up to two buttons, timing and frequency caps, and states flatly that "Pop-ups don't work with custom code" ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008375848-Creating-a-promotional-pop-up)). BentoBox offers promotion and announcement overlays with publish and unpublish dates ([BentoBox](https://help.getbento.com/en/articles/415425)), and Take App, the closest structural analogue to Quicktalog, offers an image card with an optional link, a top banner, a pop-up and a countdown card ([Take App](https://www.take.app/help/designing-storefront)). In Quicktalog's sandbox a banner's button cannot leave the frame, and in either code block a restaurant or salon owner faces a coding task. The fix is a native **Banner** block built from typed fields (image, heading, short text, up to two buttons, theme colours) that the AI can write safely and every plan can use.

"Other custom HTML" splits along the two existing trust models. Static markup that needs working links, such as a styled table or a promotion with inline styles, already renders correctly through the embed sanitizer, which keeps inline styles, images, new-tab links and `tel:` links while dropping scripts, `<style>` blocks and buttons ([sanitizer probe](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_embed_custom_code.md)). That path should become the embed block's "Custom HTML (static)" advanced entry at Pro. Interactive code stays in the sandbox, which follows MDN's warning against combining `allow-scripts` with `allow-same-origin` for same-origin content ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/iframe)) and the industry's direction: Wix sandboxed custom elements from January 2025, and Shopify stops injecting storefront script tags on 1 March 2027 ([Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/updates-to-iframes-and-custom-elements); [Shopify](https://community.shopify.dev/t/deprecation-script-tags-will-stop-running-on-march-1-2027/37108)). Rename it "Interactive widget", add a live preview rendered through the same sandbox, say plainly what it cannot do, surface the 5,000-pixel cap and self-host Monaco. One trade-off deserves an explicit decision rather than a default. Adding `allow-popups`, `allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox` or `allow-top-navigation-by-user-activation` would let widget buttons open real links without granting cookie, storage or page access, but AI-written or injected code could then send a visitor anywhere on a tap. That reasoning is untested.

## Hours, location and a tap-to-act button are the missing essentials

Visitors reach a catalogue in two modes. At the table, after scanning a QR code, the venue is already chosen and the job is to show the menu fast and legibly: diners' top complaint about QR menus is small text (26%), and 81% still prefer paper ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/qr-code-menu-insights)). From a shared link or social profile, the visitor is still deciding and wants photos, prices, reviews, hours, location and a way to act. The survey evidence is consistent across verticals, though mostly American and often vendor-sponsored. 80% of US restaurant guests visit a restaurant's website mainly to see the menu, and 74% look for special offers ([Owner.com](https://www.owner.com/blog/restaurant-website-design)). **85% of consumers call contact details and opening hours important** when researching a local business ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/consumer-search-behavior/)), and **44% had turned up at a closed venue because the hours online were wrong**, with 73% unlikely to return ([Yext](https://investors.yext.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/32/yext-research-finds-that-nearly-half-of-u-s-consumers)). 97% read reviews of local businesses ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)), and 78% of salon clients check them before booking, while more than 80% want to book on their phone ([Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/4-key-salon-consumer-trends-every-business-owner-should-know-for-2025)). Messaging is the preferred contact channel for 73.3% of consumers in a Meta-commissioned 22-market survey ([WhatsApp Business](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/resource-library/state-of-business-messaging/)), and in Serbia, Bulgaria and Greece the app that matters is Viber, at around 90% penetration ([Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/blog/most-popular-messaging-apps-by-country)).

| Visitor need | Evidence | How competitors ship it | Quicktalog today | Component to build |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening hours and "open now" | 85% rate hours and contact important; 44% arrived at a closed venue | Rare as a block: Ecwid and DISH; FineDine and Popmenu sync hours from Google | No hours field anywhere; the docs say to use a text block | Hours & Location block |
| Address and directions | 1 in 5 consumers search for local businesses in a maps app | Map blocks are common (Linktree, Taplink, Take App, Ecwid) | A map iframe through the Pro embed only | "Get directions" inside Hours & Location; live map through the embed |
| Call, message or book | 73.3% prefer messaging; over 80% of salon clients want to book by phone | Contact and messaging buttons are common (Taplink, Take App, Linktree) | One header and one footer button on Basic+; body text cannot hold a link | Buttons block, then a per-item action |
| Photos, banners and offers | 74% look for offers; item photos linked to up to 44% more sales | Image, banner and countdown cards are near-universal | No image block; banners need code on Pro+ | Banner block, then Gallery |
| Reviews | 97% read reviews; 78% of salon clients check first | Review blocks are common (Take App, Ecwid, Linktree, FineDine) | Nothing | A reviews link now, a reviews block later |
| Allergens and dietary needs | Mandatory in the EU for non-prepacked food; over a quarter of UK diners look allergens up | An item attribute (FineDine) | No field, though marketing promises one | Allergen and dietary badges on items |
| Policies and common questions | Delivery radius, cancellation, parking, allergen detail | FAQ blocks are common (Taplink, Ecwid, Linktree) | A wall of text | FAQ block |

Sources for the table beyond those above: the photo figure is DoorDash platform data, reported as "up to" and confounded ([DoorDash](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/learning-center/maximize-your-menu)); the UK allergen figure comes from search logs, not a survey ([Restaurant Online](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/04/15/over-a-quarter-of-uk-diners-check-multiple-allergens-before-eating-out/)); competitor coverage is from Ecwid's section library ([Ecwid](https://support.ecwid.com/hc/en-us/articles/11721659199388-Adding-and-editing-site-sections)), DISH's starter guide ([DISH](https://support.dish.co/website-weblisting-starter-guide/dish-website-starter-guide)), FineDine's plans ([FineDine](https://www.finedinemenu.com/en/pricing/)) and Linktree's marketplace ([Linktree](https://linktr.ee/marketplace/browse)).

None of the four first-order needs has a block today. No hours field exists anywhere in the app: the docs tell owners to type hours into a text block ([build-and-edit.tsx:109-116](../../../../../src/content/docs/build-and-edit.tsx#L109-L116)), and the agent is told to take opening hours from web pages with nowhere structured to put them ([instructions.ts:73](../../../../../src/agent/instructions.ts#L73)). The legal picture sharpens the Starter problem. The EU E-commerce Directive requires a business's name, geographic address and contact details, including email, to be "easily, directly and permanently accessible" ([Directive 2000/31/EC, Art. 5](https://lexparency.org/eu/32000L0031/ART_5/)). The Services Directive adds pre-contract information for salons and gyms, including the price where it is pre-determined ([Directive 2006/123/EC](https://lexparency.org/eu/32006L0123/)), and Serbia's e-commerce law mirrors the identity duty ([Paragraf](https://www.paragraf.rs/propisi/zakon_o_elektronskoj_trgovini.html)). Quicktalog renders a legal name and address only in the custom footer, which Starter cannot use ([CompanyInfo.tsx:5-29](../../../../../src/components/catalogue/view/components/CompanyInfo.tsx#L5-L29); [entitlements/catalogue.ts:13-28](../../../../../src/lib/entitlements/catalogue.ts#L13-L28)).

Most of what owners ask for is not a new block but an attribute of items. Wix's public request list for restaurants asks for item- and section-level availability, customer search and sort, bulk editing, and different prices for the same item in different menus ([Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/request-varying-price-for-same-item-in-different-menus-in-wix-restaurants)). Square staff called item allergens a recognised request that is "not on the immediate road-map" in December 2024 ([Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Orders-Menu-Items-Catalog/Allergens-add-to-items/m-p/687885)). For EU food businesses the allergen question is a legal one: allergen information is mandatory for non-prepacked food, must be available before purchase when food is sold remotely ([European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/food-information-consumers-legislation/distance-selling_en)), and, absent national rules, "it is not possible to provide allergen information only upon request" ([Commission Notice 2017/C 428/01](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A52017XC1213%2801%29)). Peer-reviewed menu research adds two design constraints. Showing diners the five most popular dishes raised demand for them by **13–20%** in a randomised field experiment ([Cai, Chen & Fang 2009](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.99.3.864)), while moving vegetarian dishes into their own section reduced how often they were chosen ([Bacon & Krpan 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0195666317309480)). Dietary information therefore belongs on the item, as a badge with a legend, not in a separate section, and a "popular" badge is one of the few page elements with causal evidence behind it.

Against the 24 vertical products surveyed, Quicktalog's priced-items block holds its own against the nearest equivalents, Taplink's "Price lists" block and Catalog Machine's price-list pages ([Taplink](https://taplink.at/en/blog/how-to-create-a-qr-code-menu.html)). Its allowlisted embed matches the curated model of Linktree and Lnk.bio, and its sandboxed custom code is stricter than the in-page code Carrd, Taplink and Ecwid inject. What it lacks as blocks is the category's standard set: an image or gallery, a button, a map or location, an FAQ, a promo banner, reviews and a featured-items spotlight ([Take App](https://www.take.app/help/designing-storefront); [Ecwid](https://support.ecwid.com/hc/en-us/articles/11721659199388-Adding-and-editing-site-sections)). Two gaps are genuine differentiation opportunities because competitors rarely fill them: a structured opening-hours block, which only Ecwid and DISH ship as a block, and item-level allergen badges, confirmed only at FineDine.

## The evidence backs prepared blocks, with named variants as the pressure valve

The founder's lean toward prepared solutions holds for Quicktalog's users and page shape. Six independent lines of evidence point the same way, and the counter-evidence applies mainly to conditions Quicktalog does not have.

Primitive-first tools have spent six years adding prepared layers for non-designers. Webflow added prebuilt hero, navigation and contact layouts in 2020 "to help you get pages started faster" ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/updates/build-faster-with-prebuilt-layouts)), and later a Marketer role that can only drop existing components into designer-defined page slots ([Webflow Help](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961210206483-Page-building)). Framer added pre-made Sections in 2022 ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/updates/sections)). WordPress 6.7 made patterns, rather than "lower-level, individual blocks", the default editing unit in 2024 ([WordPress](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2024/10/21/developer-notes-for-zoom-out-in-wordpress-6-7/)), after adding content-only locking because "End users may have found they could accidentally break these layouts" ([WordPress](https://make.wordpress.org/core/2022/10/11/content-locking-features-and-updates/)). From the prepared end, Shopify added preset categories in May 2025 so merchants "find the right design elements faster" ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/changelog/categories-for-section-and-block-presets)).

The two vendors that pushed freedom into simple products paid for it with their less confident users. Squarespace's free-positioning Fluid Engine drew a long-running forum revolt: "Now I am spending twice the time to do simple edits", "Why do I have to make separate designs for desktop and mobile?", "How about just making something responsive and letting it stack?" ([Squarespace Forum](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/224619-fluid-engine-is-trash/)). Shopify's Horizon theme, built on blocks that nest up to eight levels deep, stood at **35% positive across 208 reviews** on 30 September 2026 ([Shopify Theme Store](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/horizon/styles/horizon)), a figure that also reflects bugs and update churn; new merchants get confused when the setting they read about is hidden behind a toggle ([Craftshift](https://craftshift.com/shopify-horizon-theme-hidden-settings-tips-2026/)).

Free-form composition fails on phones by construction. Canva warns that "On mobile view, overlapping elements will shrink, and ungrouped elements might separate" ([Canva](https://www.canva.com/help/responsive-website-issues/)), and Hostinger's manual editor shows mobile elements in the order they were added on desktop without carrying later reorders across ([Hostinger](https://support.hostinger.com/en/articles/6475658-website-builder-how-to-edit-the-mobile-version-of-a-website)). Quicktalog's page is already a single-column list opened on a phone, so grid, flex and column primitives would import exactly these failure modes for little visual gain on a 360-pixel screen. The vertical market agrees. Nearly every menu, catalogue and link-in-bio builder surveyed uses stacked single-column blocks with named variants, such as Linktree's Stack, Grid, Carousel and Showcase collections ([Linktree](https://linktr.ee/help/en/articles/5434163-organize-your-links-with-collections)). Carrd is the only one built mostly on primitives, and Bento.me, the grid-first link-in-bio tool, shut down on 13 February 2026 and sent its users to Linktree ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/12/bento-to-shut-down-in-2026-as-linktree-takes-over-and-offers-migration-path/)).

Choice research explains why. A meta-analysis of 99 observations found that a larger assortment causes overload when four moderators are present: choice-set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty and decision goal ([Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057740814000916)). Non-technical owners score high on uncertainty and difficulty, and primitives raise both, because every layout has to be invented. AI sharpens the case rather than removing it. Schema-constrained generation reaches 100% adherence on complex JSON schemas, against under 40% without it ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-structured-outputs-in-the-api/)), so a closed set of typed blocks can be generated, validated on the server, diffed and undone. Shopify's Sidekick edits the settings of sections a theme already has and does not write new section types ([Fudge.ai](https://www.fudge.ai/guides/shopify-sidekick-use-cases/)), while Shopify says troubleshooting the code of its AI-generated blocks "isn't supported by Shopify Support" ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/customizing-themes/theme-editor/shopify-magic/generate-blocks)). Time is the final constraint: **70% of small-business owners spend under five hours a week on marketing** ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/news/small-business-survey-2025)), and while Wix reports that average time to publish fell from eight days to four in a year of AI tooling, 91% of its users still rewrote the AI's copy and nearly 70% replaced its images ([Wix](https://www.wix.com/blog/how-is-ai-changing-website-creation)). Time-to-value is content work, not layout work.

The counter-evidence is real but conditional. Shopify built Horizon because "Merchants tell us they want more expression, more control" ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/summer-25-edition-design)). Prepared-only builders are criticised as rigid, as when Durable lets users remove form fields but not add them ([Tooltester](https://www.tooltester.com/en/reviews/durable/)). Some Fluid Engine users produce better sites with it ([Squarespace Forum](https://forum.squarespace.com/topic/224619-fluid-engine-is-trash/)), AI is cutting the setup cost of primitives through tools such as Wix Harmony and Framer's Wireframer ([Wix](https://www.wix.com/press-room/home/post/wix-launches-wix-harmony-the-ai-website-builder-that-merges-human-and-artificial-intelligence-rein); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250521574932/en/Framer-Launches-AI-Features-to-Supercharge-Web-Design-Democratizing-How-Stunning-Websites-are-Built)), and the average choice-overload effect across 50 experiments is close to zero, so a larger picker is not harmful if it is well organised ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Can-There-Ever-Be-Too-Many-Options-A-Meta-Analytic-Scheibehenne-Greifeneder/07775d812c00a97aaa57b55596b39e26b8baaa0b)). These arguments win where users are designers or agencies, brand differentiation is the product, pages are desktop-first and content rarely changes. Quicktalog's owners are the opposite on every count: non-technical and short of time, editing prices and hours often, publishing to a phone-first single column, in verticals with predictable content types, with an AI agent in the loop.

"Prepared" must not mean "rigid", and the same evidence shows how to avoid it. First, show one block type as several named picker entries with different defaults, as Shopify presets and WordPress's Group, Row, Stack and Grid variations do ([WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/group-block/)), and let owners switch variants without losing content, as Squarespace's auto layouts do ([Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057763852-Auto-layouts)); the items block's four layouts already work this way. Second, let catalogue-wide theme tokens own styling and give each block only a variant switch plus a few presentation tokens, such as full width or a themed background band spanning consecutive blocks, which Taplink calls sections ([Taplink](https://taplink.at/en/guide/sections.html)). Third, compose blocks internally from sub-parts if that helps the team, but never expose the tree to owners, which is what WordPress's content-only locking and Webflow's Marketer role enforce. Fourth, let the AI agent create and edit every block through a per-type typed schema; today its update tool is a fixed field list, so any new structured block would be create-and-delete only for the AI ([operations.ts:265-305](../../../../../src/lib/catalogue/operations.ts#L265-L305)). Fifth, meet the ceiling with a fast internal pipeline for new block types and variants, plus a small "Advanced" group of escape hatches, and never by switching everyone to a free-form mode, which was Squarespace's mistake.

## Twelve moves in four waves give the fastest time to value

The ordering rule is simple: first fix what currently misrepresents a business, then answer the visitor's first questions on every plan, then enrich pages, then integrate. Within each wave, the strength of visitor-need evidence and its breadth across segments rank the work, and owner effort and build cost break ties. The end state is **ten block types in five picker groups**: Items (Catalogue); Hours & Location and Buttons (Contact & actions); Banner, Gallery and the Embed entries (Media); Text, FAQ and Divider/Spacer (Content); and the static-HTML and Interactive widget entries (Advanced). Around them sit three item upgrades and one catalogue setting. In the size column, S is about a sitting, M a few and L a week or more.

| 2 | Text with links, H2/H3, clean paste and working sizes | Block upgrade | 0 | All | S | The sanitizer already allows it; unlocks body links everywhere |
| 3 | Hours & Location: weekly hours with split shifts, holiday note, client-side "open now", address with "Get directions", call and message buttons, structured data | New block | 1 | All | M | A first-order need in every segment; rare among competitors |

Plan placement follows the category's convention: basic content free, richer media on the first paid tier, embeds, custom code and tracking at the middle, commerce and reviews at the top. Taplink gives links, text, maps and FAQ away, adds images and video at $4 and forms and payments at $8 ([Taplink](https://taplink.at/en/blog/what-is-taplink.html)); Carrd puts forms, widgets, embeds and tracking in Pro Standard ([Carrd](https://carrd.com/docs/pro/plans)); and FineDine includes allergen labels on every plan ([FineDine](https://www.finedinemenu.com/en/pricing/)). This makes Starter far more useful and moves Basic's pitch from "a divider and your own contact details" to branding, a gallery, an FAQ, embeds and per-item actions. Plan prices and customer distribution by plan are not in the code, so the placement is a proposal for the founder to test, not a finding.

### Wave 0: stop the product misrepresenting the business

The first wave adds no block types. Show "Free" only when an item is explicitly marked free and show no price otherwise: the editor already sets an `isFree` flag when an owner ticks "Free", the renderer ignores it, and the card already handles a missing price. Tell the AI to set that flag for free items instead of writing 0 for unknown prices, and show the sale price and unit in the detail modal. The full price modes ("from", "on request") follow in Wave 1, because making `price` optional is a breaking change to the shared package. Add link, heading and clean-paste support to the text toolbar, and map the size picker to markup the sanitizer keeps. Make the divider follow the theme, move it to Starter and split it into Spacer and Divider entries. Wire the unused insert-at-position helper to a "+" between blocks, align the "External content" and "Embedding" labels, and fix the agent's stale type list, which still says "Use 'category' for items" although its schema accepts only `items` ([instructions.ts:42](../../../../../src/agent/instructions.ts#L42); [schemas.ts:9-28](../../../../../src/agent/schemas.ts#L9-L28)). Alongside, lay the foundation every later block needs: one block registry replacing the roughly 18 hand-maintained type lists; a server-side check of block type and shape on save and publish, which grandfathers only types already present in the stored row, since block ids are generated in the browser and can be forged; one section-counting rule; and a single host list that generates both the sanitizer allowlist and CSP `frame-src`, defusing the 36-host outage before anyone switches the CSP to enforcing.

### Wave 1: answer the visitor's first questions on every plan

Hours & Location, Buttons and Banner ship together with price modes and allergen badges. Hours & Location should read the catalogue's contact data so a phone number is maintained in one place, and compute "open now" in the browser after the page loads, because the public page is cached for a day ([page.tsx:11](../../../../../src/app/catalogues/%5Bname%5D/page.tsx#L11)). It should default to a "Get directions" link rather than a map iframe, which avoids a third-party request, a consent question and page weight. It should also emit LocalBusiness structured data, which catalogue routes currently lack entirely, and render day names in the catalogue's language, which needs better defaults: all seven showcases are set to English although at least four are written in Spanish, Serbian or French ([showcase parse](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_blocks_inventory.md)). Buttons should build `tel:`, WhatsApp and Viber links from validated numbers rather than accept author-supplied URLs.

For these blocks to work on Starter, Starter owners must be able to enter their own phone number and address. Unlock the contact basics on every plan, and keep the logo, the custom header and footer, the SEO fields and removal of Quicktalog's promotion as the Basic upgrade. That gives up one upgrade lever, but the lever currently works by making the free product fail the visitor, and for EU businesses the law. Counting also needs a decision before launch, or Starter's five sections fill up with a banner, hours and buttons before the first menu category. The cleaner rule is the one the limits dialog already implies when it tells owners they can "create N categories" ([limitContent.ts:128-139](../../../../../src/components/modals/limits/limitContent.ts#L128-L139)): count item sections toward the plan limit and give non-item blocks their own published cap. Its cost is a one-time extra slot for accounts already at their cap that hold text blocks. Counting everything and raising Starter from five sections to eight is equally defensible and keeps a single number. The same wave should replace the three generic templates with vertical starters (café menu, salon price list, shop catalogue) pre-filled with the new blocks, since Canva raised activation by 10% by routing new users to templates that matched their stated intent ([Appcues](https://www.appcues.com/blog/canva-growth-process)). It should also give the agent per-type create and edit access to every new block, because the AI chat, fed by photo OCR on every plan, is Quicktalog's fastest path to a first draft ([limits.ts:21-36](../../../../../src/lib/ai/limits.ts#L21-L36)).

### Wave 2: richer pages without code

Rebuild the embed as URL-first with named entries and click-to-load, and move the curated entries to Basic. Add Gallery and FAQ, feeding FAQ text into catalogue search, which today looks only at item names and descriptions. Then add highlight badges and the per-item action. The action needs the card fixed first: every item card is a focusable `article` with a click handler and no keyboard handler, so a button inside it would become the only keyboard-reachable part of the card ([useCard.ts:3-15](../../../../../src/hooks/useCard.ts#L3-L15)). By now the picker holds a dozen entries plus the embed presets. Flat pickers work up to about 20 types in this category, as Carrd, Take App and Taplink show, so grouping is not urgent, but group it by job (Catalogue, Contact & actions, Media, Content, Advanced) and show the entries recommended for the owner's business type first. Larger libraries group by job-to-be-done, as Linktree and Beacons do, which matches NN/g's progressive-disclosure advice to "show users only a few of the most important options" first ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)).

### Wave 3: integrations, the widget sandbox and the long tail

Ship the catalogue consent banner, then the GA4 and Meta Pixel setting. Rename custom code to "Interactive widget" with a preview and templates. Add a reviews element, starting with a "See our reviews" button to the owner's Google profile and only later curated testimonials, because Serbia's 2026 consumer law prohibits presenting reviews as coming from real customers without verification measures ([Stanković Law](https://stankoviclaw.com/compliance/novi-zakon-zastita-potrosaca-srbija-2026-trgovci/)). Price variants (S/M/L, or 30/60/90 minutes) and a class schedule for gyms come next; until the schedule exists, the marketing copy that promises class timetables should change.

Several requests should stay unbuilt. Columns, tabs and nested layout would break the flat content array, the section count, one-step reordering and the agent's one-line-per-block snapshot at once ([session.ts:375-419](../../../../../src/agent/session.ts#L375-L419)), for little benefit on a phone. Social-feed blocks depend on expiring platform tokens and live data on a page cached for a day, so they rot. Countdowns and date-scheduled blocks fight the same cache unless visibility is computed in the browser. Nothing in the demand research asks for a Lottie animation block, and it carries a real cost: lottie-web's expression support runs through `eval` and the `Function` constructor ([lottie-web #323](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie-web/issues/323)), and the CSP's `'unsafe-eval'` means even an enforced policy would not stop an animation file from executing code, unless a player with expressions disabled is used. A contact or lead form needs a route, bot protection, storage and a data-retention policy, so it is its own project; a Buttons link to the owner's existing form or a Google Forms embed covers it meanwhile. GTM and free-form head code wait on the separate-domain decision.

All of this ordering rests on external evidence, because Quicktalog's own usage is unmeasured. A read-only production query, already drafted, would classify every embed and custom-code block by host and by markers such as `<script`, `gtag(`, `fbq(` and `googletagmanager`, and every text block by hours, address and contact patterns, per plan ([query design](../research_notes/Quicktalog%20content%20block%20strategy/codebase_embed_custom_code.md)). Two answers would change priorities: how many owners hand-type hours and phone numbers into text, which would confirm Wave 1, and how many custom-code blocks hold tracking snippets that silently died when the sandbox arrived on 15 September 2026, a support problem to handle before any tracking feature ships.

## Conclusion

The useful way to frame this decision is neither "how many blocks" nor "primitives versus prepared" but where each job's trust boundary sits. Every job that moves out of the two code paths into a typed block becomes something the server can validate, the AI can edit, the consent banner can govern, a translation pass can cover and a lower plan can safely include. Every job left in code stays unvalidated, opaque to the agent's snapshot and plan-gated by necessity. Seen that way, the founder's prepared direction is also Quicktalog's security roadmap: Banner, Buttons and Hours & Location remove most of the reasons anyone would paste code, and the escape hatches can shrink to a URL embed, a static-HTML option and an honestly labelled widget sandbox.

Two cheap decisions matter more than any new block. Letting a price be absent instead of "Free", and letting Starter owners publish their own phone number and address, stop the product from misrepresenting the businesses it hosts; for EU businesses, the second is also a legal duty. The genuinely strategic fork is hosting. GTM, head code and richer third-party scripts all depend on serving public catalogues from a separate registrable domain. Until that choice is made, "tracking IDs we render ourselves" is the ceiling for integrations, and it is a defensible product position rather than a gap to apologise for.
